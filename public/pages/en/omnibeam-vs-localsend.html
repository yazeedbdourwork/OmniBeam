OmniBeam vs LocalSend

LocalSend is a fast, open-source app that sends files to nearby devices on the same network. OmniBeam does the same in the web browser, so there is nothing to install, and it also works when your devices are on different networks.

When LocalSend is better

If you move large files every day between your own computers on one network, or you often have no internet at all (a hotspot without data, a trip), LocalSend is excellent: it is native and works fully offline.

When OmniBeam is better

  • The other device isn't yours: a friend's phone, a work PC or a school computer where you can't install apps. Just open a website.
  • Your phone is on mobile data and your PC is at home: paired devices connect across the internet.
  • You want to send to someone who isn't online right now: share an encrypted link.
  • You want something quick on a new device, without hunting for the app in a store first.

Speed on the same Wi‑Fi

On one network both send directly between the devices, so speed depends mostly on your Wi‑Fi. If OmniBeam seems slow, see how to fix slow transfers on the same Wi‑Fi.

Questions and answers

Which one is easier for someone else to use?

OmniBeam, if the other person has never used either: they only need to open a web page, with nothing to install. With LocalSend, they first have to download and install the app on their device.

Which one works without internet?

LocalSend works on a local network even with no internet connection at all, which is useful in some places. OmniBeam needs an internet connection to introduce the two devices, even when the file itself then travels directly over the Wi‑Fi.

Can I send between different networks?

With OmniBeam, yes: pair the devices once with a code and they find each other from then on, even when one is on mobile data. LocalSend is designed for devices on the same network.

Are both free?

Yes. Both are free to use. LocalSend is open source; OmniBeam is free with no account and no ads.

Which one is faster?

On the same Wi‑Fi both send directly between the devices, so the speed is similar and depends on your network. OmniBeam can also continue a transfer that was interrupted, which helps with large files on a weak signal.

Which one should I choose?

If you send files only between your own devices at home and are happy to install an app on each one, LocalSend is a good choice. If you often send to other people, use computers where you cannot install apps, or need to send across different networks, OmniBeam is more convenient.

Can I use both?

Yes. They do not conflict, so you can keep LocalSend for home and use OmniBeam when you are away from your own network or sending to someone new.
