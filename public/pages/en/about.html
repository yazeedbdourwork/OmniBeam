About OmniBeam, a free tool for sending files

OmniBeam is a free, zero-install tool for sending files and text between your devices, near or far.

Moving a photo from a phone to a laptop should not need a cable, an account or an app. OmniBeam works in the browser you already have: open the site on two devices and send. It works between iPhone, Android, Windows, Mac and Linux, in English, Arabic, French, Spanish, Turkish, German, Portuguese, Hindi, Indonesian, Russian, Japanese, Chinese, Korean, Italian and Urdu.

How it works

OmniBeam uses WebRTC, the same technology as browser video calls, to connect your devices directly and encrypt the data end to end. The OmniBeam server only introduces the devices to each other. When a direct connection is impossible, the server relays the transfer, still end-to-end encrypted and without storing it.

To send a file to someone who isn't online at the same time, you can also share it by link: the file is encrypted in your browser before it is uploaded, and the key is only in the link you share.

Who makes it

OmniBeam is made by Yazeed Bdour. For feedback, ideas or bug reports, send a message on Facebook.

Questions and answers

Why was OmniBeam made?

Moving a photo or a document between your own phone and computer should take seconds, but it often means cables, emails to yourself or a cloud upload. OmniBeam was made to remove those steps: open the same page on both devices and send. It also had to work between different brands, so an iPhone and a Windows PC or an Android phone and a Mac can share files as easily as two Apple devices.

Does OmniBeam cost anything?

No. Sending files, text and links between devices is free, with no account and no ads. The project is kept running by the people who use it; if it saves you time, you can support it with the heart button at the top of the page, but nothing is locked behind a payment. As a thank-you, people who donate can get a supporter code: typed in Settings, it adds a ♥ to their device’s card that others see, four colour themes and extra pictures.

Which devices and browsers work?

Any recent browser on any device: Safari on iPhone and iPad, Chrome or Samsung Internet on Android, and Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari on Windows, Mac, Linux and Chromebook. There are also optional apps for people who prefer them: OmniBeam in the Microsoft Store for Windows, a Chrome extension, and an Android app that adds OmniBeam to the share menu.

What happens to my files?

They go from one device to the other, encrypted end to end, and are not stored anywhere on the way. When a direct connection is not possible, an encrypted relay passes the data along without being able to read it. Only files you choose to share by link are kept, encrypted, until the link expires.

How can I help the project?

Tell a friend who struggles with sending files, leave a rating in the Microsoft Store or the Chrome Web Store, or send feedback when something does not work as you expect. Reports of a phone or browser that has trouble connecting are especially useful, because they help make transfers more reliable for everyone.

Who is behind OmniBeam?

OmniBeam is an independent project made by Yazeed Bdour, not by a large company. It is built and tested on real phones and computers, and it grows from the questions and suggestions of the people who use it. That is also why the guides and articles are written in 15 languages: the people sending files every day speak many of them.

Will OmniBeam stay free?

Yes. The core of OmniBeam, sending files and text between your devices, is meant to stay free for everyone. Donations help pay for the servers that introduce devices to each other and relay transfers when a direct connection is not possible.
